Where do I come from, where am I going...

I think, learn, build, question.
Between Cameroon and Germany.
Between art, technology, history, and the future.

About me

My name is Salomon Firmin Owona and I was born in Cameroon in 1981.
My earliest memories are of the sunny days in Yaoundé:
the taste of red earth when I played in the yard,
the electric shock when I curiously experimented with the socket,
my mom coming home tired but smiling from work,
my sister talking to her friend for hours.
We were a cheerful bunch, always looking for
new adventures.

In 1989 I came to Germany with my mother and siblings
— to my father and my sister Patricia.

I didn't really want to leave Cameroon,
but my sister Ginette lured me with a
bicycle and convinced me.
I remember exactly that cold
winter morning in Erkrath-Hochdahl.
Everything was sterile, so foreign — no colors,
no warmth.
Everyone looked at us as if we were not from
this world.
My mom was not treated well at the airport,
the officers' glances were cold and dismissive.
It was very cold, not just the weather —
an emotional coldness that I will never forget.
Nevertheless, there was also this excitement — starting a new life.

Arriving was not a single event.
It was a long, sometimes painful process.
I learned to adapt, to build bridges
and at the same time to preserve my roots.

I grew up in Erkrath-Hochdahl.
Elementary School Sandheide, where I found my first friends —
but also where I experienced my first real fight.
I can remember a blow to the head,
after I insisted that a writing utensil belonged to me.
That was apparently not accepted by someone.
I was unconscious. I just don't know for how long.
I never told my parents about it.
Realschule Hochdahl — a place full of trauma.
My history teacher had instructed another student,
a few classes above me, Indian or Pakistani (I don't remember anymore),
to hit me.
I was already strong and well-trained for my age.
The teacher was present. I just stood there.
The situation was very disturbing.
The same teacher had beaten up a boy in the hallway during the same school year.
That was the 90s at the Realschule.
Nevertheless, I started asking questions there.
Later, the Higher Commercial School in Hilden,
where I learned that knowledge is power.

The annual summer vacations as a child were varied.
Sometimes I went to a youth camp,
or I just wandered from basketball hoop to basketball hoop with friends.
It was never boring.
Stories of flights to exotic islands or to Gran Canaria
I never had to tell like other children.
But that was okay.
Only strange that none of these wealthy classmates
knew the African continent.
What accompanied me early on was self-learning.
Not as a glamorous choice, but as pure necessity.
When you look different and speak differently,
you have to be better to keep up.
From this necessity became passion –
the joy of discovering, understanding, growing.

I observe, test, understand.
Whether music, technology, trade, or people —
everything is a big experiment to me.
Why do people do what they do?
What drives them? What holds them back?
These questions have occupied me for years.

I like jogging around the swimming lake.
Not for fitness, but for clarity.
With every step, I sort thoughts,
check my stats on the sports watch,
and ask myself: Am I still on the right path?

I am a father of three children:
Afidi, Otis, and Salomon Jr.
They are my anchor in this unsteady world.
When everything around me changes,
they remind me of what really counts:
Love, laughter, unconditional acceptance.
They ground me more than any concept,
any philosophy, any success.

I would go through fire for my family,
because I know they would do the same for me.
I am difficult because I am the youngest among the siblings,
but still loud — in African cultures, younger ones don't have much to say.
Nevertheless! I have always raised my voice,
spoken my opinion, followed my dreams.
That has shaped me: respectful towards elders,
but never silent in the face of injustice.

What I do

I have never done just one thing.

Early years until about 30 years old

Rap & Music – Finding my voice

In my early years it was rap – not just music, but therapy.
Texts about identity, belonging, the pain of being in between.
My artist name was Mr. F.o.b.s. My initials.
During my training I got to know the Pforzheim underground.
With the guys from Mühlacker I shot my first music video "Fobsheim".
A Pforzheimer gave me the idea – "Fobs' home", nice wordplay.
I remember that magical performance in 2003 in Stuttgart Sillenbuch:
I asked for the mic to be brought to the back exit.
Starting from off: "Mic-check 1-2, Stuttgart, are you ready say hoooh!!"
They didn't know who was coming. Rapping from all the way back on the way to the stage
suddenly I had a lot behind me,
and an even larger crowd eager to find out who I am.
Around the corner rapping over a basketball dribble to the basket –
they saw me and started cheering. That was magical.
Later music production: building beats in my small room,
stages, performances, the adrenaline of the spotlight.
I was regularly on the road live and locally known –
for the first time I was really seen.

2002 – 2005

Media designer – Technology as language

Training at the Evangelical Broadcasting Service Baden in Karlsruhe.
Here I learned to tell stories through image and sound.
Technology became my tool for transporting stories.
For the first time I felt really competent in professional life.

2007

Live moderation – The entertainer

For several months live moderator at NRW TV.
Suddenly I was in front of cameras, conducting interviews,
making people laugh, think.
A role that suited me – the mediator, the bridge builder.

2018 – 2020

Trade & E-Commerce – Business with psychology

Amazon FBA became my university of trade.
Bestseller products, import/export, Amazon SEO, listings.
But above all: sales psychology.
Why do people buy? What drives decisions?
I learned more about human nature than in any study.

Since 2017

Crypto & Decentralization – The search for freedom

Crypto was never hype for me – it was a philosophical question.
What happens when money is no longer controlled by institutions?
When power is decentralized?
I dove deep: reading whitepapers at night,
building communities, conducting philosophical discussions.
It was my search for a fairer system.

Since 2024

Vibe Coding & SaaS – The digital forge

Now I build digital worlds: SaaS products,
including whatsapp.owona.de.
Code as art form, products as solutions to problems.
Every day the joy of creating something from nothing,
that helps others improve their lives.

Parallel for years

Ndasi.com – Roots and future

Management of Ndasi.com in Cameroon – my contribution at home.
Production, import of brick-making machines.
Not development aid, but construction.
Creating jobs, transferring knowledge,
empowering the next generation.
Because I know what it's like to come from outside.

Values & Attitude

I am active against racism.
Not out of conviction alone — but because I experienced it every day.
Since my youth in Germany, where "foreigner" was still a swear word.

Racism is not a fringe issue.
It structures life paths, destroys dreams, builds invisible walls.
It decided which schools I was allowed to attend,
which jobs I got, which doors opened.
And it made me stronger — more resilient, more empathetic, more determined.

That is why I work with friends on worldwide online communities
like the Black Lives Memorial Heidelberg.
We make Black history visible,
share knowledge, build bridges between cultures.
Because remembrance is the key to change.

I believe in education as a weapon, context as understanding,
memory as a foundation.
And that technology and history must be thought of together —
because algorithms reflect the prejudices of their creators,
and because digital spaces must be just as fair as physical ones.
