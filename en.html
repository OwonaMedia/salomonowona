About Me

My name is Salomon Firmin Owona and I was born in 1981 in Cameroon.
My earliest memories are of sunny days in Yaoundé:
the taste of the red earth when I played in the yard,
the electric shock when I curiously experimented with a socket,
my Mama coming home tired but smiling from work,
my sister talking to her friend for hours.
We were a cheerful gang, always looking for
new adventures.

In 1989, I came to Germany with my mother and siblings
– to my father and my sister Patricia.

I actually didn't want to leave Cameroon,
but my sister Ginette lured and convinced me with a
bicycle.
I still remember that cold winter morning in Erkrath-Hochdahl vividly.
Everything was so sterile, so foreign – no colors,
no warmth.
Everyone looked at us as if we were not from
this world.
My Mama was not treated well at the airport,
the officials' glances were cold and rejecting.
It was very cold, not just the weather –
an emotional coldness I will never forget.
Despite that, there was also this excitement – starting a new life.

Arriving was not an event.
It was a long, sometimes painful process.
I learned to adapt, build bridges,
and preserve my roots at the same time.

I grew up in Erkrath-Hochdahl.
Primary school Sandheide, where I found my first friends –
but also where I experienced my first real fight.
I remember a blow to the head
after I insisted that a writing utensil belonged to me.
That was apparently not accepted by someone.
I was unconscious. I just don't know for how long.
I never told my parents about it.
Realschule Hochdahl – a place full of trauma.
My history teacher had another student,
a few classes above me, Indian or Pakistani (I don't remember anymore),
order me to be beaten.
I was already strong and well-trained for my age.
The teacher was present. I just stood there.
The situation was very disturbing.
The same teacher beat up a boy in the hallway in the same school year.
Those were the 90s at the Realschule.
Nevertheless, I started asking questions there.
Later the Höhere Handelsschule (Higher Commercial School) in Hilden,
where I learned that knowledge is power.

The annual summer holidays as a child were diverse.
Occasionally it was off to a holiday camp,
or I simply wandered from basketball hoop to basketball hoop with friends.
It never got boring.
Stories of flights to exotic islands or to Gran Canaria
I never had to tell like other children.
But that was okay.
Just weird that none of these wealthy classmates
knew the African continent.
What accompanied me early on was self-learning.
Not as a glamorous choice, but as pure necessity.
When you look different and speak differently,
you have to be better to keep up.
From this necessity became passion –
the joy of discovery, understanding, growing.

I observe, test, understand.
Whether music, technology, trade, or people –
everything is a big experiment for me.
Why do people do what they do?
What drives them? What holds them back?
These questions have occupied me for years.

I like to jog around the Badesee.
Not for fitness, but for clarity.
With every step, I sort thoughts,
check my values on my sports watch
and ask myself: Am I still on the right path?

I am the father of three children:
Afidi, Otis and Salomon Jr.
They are my anchor in this unstable world.
When everything around me changes,
they remind me of what really counts:
Love, laughter, unconditional acceptance.
They ground me more than any concept,
any philosophy, any success.

I would go through fire for my family,
because I know they would do the same for me.
I am inconvenient because I am the youngest among the siblings,
but still loud – in African cultures, the young don't have much to say.
Nevertheless! I have always raised my voice,
stated my opinion, pursued my dreams.
This shaped me: respectful of elders,
but never silent in the face of injustice.

What I Do

I have never done just one thing.

Early Years to ~30th Year

Rap & Music – Finding My Voice

In my early years, it was Rap – not just music, but therapy.
Lyrics about identity, belonging, the pain of being in-between.
My artist name was Mr. F.o.b.s. My initials.
During my training, I got to know the Pforzheim underground.
With the guys from Mühlacker, I shot my first music video "Fobsheim".
A guy from Pforzheim gave me the idea – "Fobs' Heim", a nice play on words.
I remember this magical appearance in 2003 in Stuttgart Sillenbuch:
I asked for the mic to be brought to the back exit.
Starting from off-stage: "Mic-check 1-2, Stuttgart, are you ready say hoooh!!"
They didn't know who was coming. Rapping from the very back on the way to the stage
I suddenly had a lot behind my back,
and an even larger mob eager to find out who I am.
Rapping around the corner over a basketball cart to the hoop –
they saw me and started cheering. That was magical.
Later music production: building beats in my small room,
stages, appearances, the adrenaline of the spotlight.
I was regularly live on the road and locally known –
the first time I was really seen.

2002 – 2005

Media Designer – Technology as Language

Apprenticeship at the Evangelischer Rundfunkdienst Baden in Karlsruhe.
Here I learned to tell images, to shape sounds.
Technology became my tool to transport stories.
For the first time, I felt truly competent in professional life.

2007

Live Moderation – The Entertainer

Live moderator at NRW TV for several months.
Suddenly I stood in front of cameras, conducted interviews,
made people laugh, think.
A role that suited me – the mediator, the bridge builder.

2018 – 2020

Trade & E-Commerce – The Business of Psychology

Amazon FBA became my university of commerce.
Bestseller products, Import/Export, Amazon SEO, Listings.
But above all: Sales Psychology.
Why do people buy? What drives decisions?
I learned more about human nature than in any study.

Since 2017

Crypto & Decentralization – The Search for Freedom

Crypto was never hype for me – it was a philosophical question.
What happens when money is no longer controlled by institutions?
When power is decentralized?
I dove deep: reading whitepapers at night,
building communities, leading philosophical discussions.
It was my search for a fairer system.

Since 2024

Vibe Coding & SaaS – The Digital Forge

Now I build digital worlds: SaaS products,
including whatsapp.owona.de.
Code as art form, products as problem solutions.
Every day the joy of creating something from nothing
that helps others improve their lives.

Parallel for years

Ndasi.com – Roots and Future

Management of Ndasi.com in Cameroon – my contribution at home.
Production, import of machines for brick production.
Not development aid, but building up.
Creating jobs, transferring knowledge,
empowering the next generation.
Because I know what it's like to come from the outside.

Values & Stance

I am actively against racism.
Not out of conviction alone – but because I experience it every day.
Since my youth in Germany, where "foreigner" was still a swear word.

Racism is not a fringe topic.
It structures life paths, destroys dreams, builds invisible walls.
It decided which schools I was allowed to attend,
which jobs I got, which doors opened.
And it made me stronger – more resilient, more empathetic, more determined.

That's why I work with friends on global online communities
like the Black Lives Memorial Heidelberg.
We make Black history visible,
share knowledge, build bridges between cultures.
Because memory is the key to change.

I believe in education as a weapon, context as understanding,
memory as foundation.
And that technology and history must be thought together –
because algorithms reflect the prejudices of their creators,
and because digital spaces must be just as fair as physical ones.
